### Quantified checks performed directly on the stored CI model JSON (not new source outputs)

- Among the **1,077** originally feasible five-site sets, only **8** have **a single connected new-site-only subgraph**, while 129 have five new-site-only singleton components, 461 have four components, 384 have three, and 95 have two. **Do not interpret this as 1,069 physically disconnected systems**: existing stations may provide links not represented by the new-only subgraph, and the paper may intend local access rather than global connectivity. It does establish that the model does **not require a connected new-only network**.
- Original selected best is **−3.3012934918564643** and second-best original locally feasible five-site selection is **−3.1020113800145808**, a gap **0.1992821118418835** *for the unchanged model and candidate set*. This is much larger than observed 1-ULP CPU drift, **but not** a sensitivity bound for wrong weights, missing data or hydrodynamic assumptions.
- **Illustrative inadmissible counterfactual, not a scientific alternative:** dropping the one-hop constraint while keeping the same five-site budget and objective gives best **−3.55912519980019**, selecting `[1,2,5,16,18]`; this picks **four** currently unreachable candidates. It quantifies how much the original connectivity filter can matter but does **not** justify removing a real deployment constraint.
- **Illustrative posthoc formula sensitivity, not a pre-registered paper result:** removing only the source-defined wake contribution (reconstructed from recorded free/fixed trace and source free/free coordinates) retains **[0,1,3,6,7]** as best for this case, with original objective recalculated as approximately **−3.302471040188822** under that *new* objective. Wake's numerical effect on this winner is about **0.001178**, and a genuinely directed-current model would be a different analysis. This does not validate the proxy physically.

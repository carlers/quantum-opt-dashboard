# Pre-registered repeated-seed N=20 classical SA replication

**Status: analysis plan frozen before the extended experiment.** Tracking [issue #22](https://github.com/carlers/quantum-opt-dashboard/issues/22); upstream exploratory [issue #20](https://github.com/carlers/quantum-opt-dashboard/issues/20) and exact QUBO [issue #18](https://github.com/carlers/quantum-opt-dashboard/issues/18). The experiment is limited to the committed notebook-weight Laguna de Bay sensor-placement input. This is **not** historical-paper QUBO parity, proof of global connectivity, or a quantum-computing result.

## Fixed protocol

- Comparison: same N=20 free candidate sites, K=5 selected new sites, 15 existing stations, objective coefficients, one-hop adjacency constraint and fully certified SCIP optimum as upstream, **−3.3012934918564643**. Repeat independent 15,504-subset enumeration on the unchanged source.
- Classical sampler: `openjij==0.12.2`, `SASampler`, **300 sweeps** per call, separate `num_reads=1` calls with distinct 32-bit child seeds generated by `np.random.SeedSequence(parent).spawn(32)`. Preserve per-read results, initial parent, child-seed and read-index metadata.
- New parent seeds **270010001–270010032**, inclusive, all disjoint from upstream pilot's 20261018–20261020 and test-only 1970–1971. Each of **32 parent blocks** has **32 child reads per penalty**. Exactly **3,072** total draws with 1,024 draws per penalty.
- Three penalties exactly as before: **notebook local row-sum heuristic** (not globally sufficient), **exploratory midpoint**, **globally sufficient 2B+1**, where `B=sum |a_i|+sum|Q_ij|`. Same penalty on budget squared and per-node squared local connectivity with auxiliary slacks. **No tuning after inspecting output.**
- The **primary endpoint** is probability that an original (x-only) state is feasible and within absolute energy `1e-7` of the verified SCIP optimum; all draws, including infeasible ones, remain in denominator. Secondary endpoint is original feasibility fraction; also report best/mean feasible raw objective and original energy gaps, with no-finite-result represented as `null`.
- Paired contrasts: **midpoint − row-sum** and **global − row-sum** for both endpoints, reported as **percentage points** (positive indicates higher rate for named comparator). Use matching parent seeds as natural blocks across the three penalties.
- **Descriptive 95% percentile paired-block bootstrap:** PRNG seed **734021**, **10,000** draws of **32 parent blocks with replacement**; within each draw reuse the same selected block indices for all three penalties and both metrics, rather than bootstrapping individual reads. Return bootstrap 2.5th–97.5th percentiles for per-penalty rates and paired rate differences, with explicit `bootstrap_unit=parent_seed`. Do not claim independent environmental experimental units or corrected family-wise inference.
- Existing thin pilot is exploratory. No data-dependent selection of seeds, reads, penalty values, sweeps, outcomes or intervals. CI may rerun the identical protocol for code fixes only, and the revision/history of any fixes must remain visible.

## Planned source/artifact contract

`sa_paired_replication.py` batches eight parents at a time through the independently verified `sa_benchmark.run_benchmark` runner to keep the original pilot's 10-seed bounded safety limit unchanged. Each batch reconstructs/independently verifies the same SCIP optimum and source hashes. The follow-up writes:

- `protocol.json` — frozen config, objective and dataset/notebook provenance, sampler version, and seed manifest.
- `summary.json` — counts and percentages, conditional raw objective summary, 10,000-iteration paired bootstrap intervals and contrasts.
- `trials.csv` — 32×3=96 parent×penalty records with 32 reads per row.
- `samples.csv` — all 3,072 encoded states and complete seed indices, including infeasible draws.
- `manifest.json` — exact SHA-256 checksum for each of the other four files.

CI must compile Python, pass synthetic-statistics tests and upstream solver/encoder/sampler tests, generate this artifact without exceeding its fixed computational ceiling, and explicitly verify its output. A successful result is **an experiment result on one fixed instance**, not proof that the chosen penalties or classical SA generalize to N≥50, evacuation-center siting, other lake geographies, JijModeling's unknown old encoding, or any quantum method.

The live Streamlit branch and original notebook/dataset are frozen pending explicit user approval for a deployment-changing merge.

Every confidence-style interval above is a *descriptive* percentile bootstrap using 10,000 **paired parent-seed blocks** and one fixed N=20 objective instance. They are not multiplicity-adjusted hypothesis tests, and do not establish what happens across changed lake data, weight settings or candidate densities. **The observed midpoint optimum-hit rate was zero**, so its naive percentile bootstrap interval is exactly `[0,0]`: this is a *degenerate observed-sample bootstrap*, **not** evidence that its true chance of finding an optimum is exactly zero. Do not interpret it as a calibrated coverage statement for rare events.

The larger experiment supports a trade-off in these settings: row-sum sampled exact optima more often but yielded more original-infeasible placements; both larger penalties increased original-feasible sampling. The globally sufficient penalty's theorem applies to a **global minimum of its encoded QUBO**, whereas these are finite-time, independently seeded local stochastic trajectories.

## Cross-run reproducibility audit — scientifically important limitation (2026-10-18)

**Issue [#24](https://github.com/carlers/quantum-opt-dashboard/issues/24) is now OPEN.** The CI implementation succeeds, but its nominally fixed input and child seed manifest produced **different row-sum feasibility outcomes** on independent GitHub Actions runners. **This changes the scientific interpretation:** the first observed result above is an archived outcome, **not** a guarantee of bitwise repeatability.

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| [39096889778](https://github.com/carlers/quantum-opt-dashboard/actions/runs/39096889778) | **698** | 13 | 790 | 790 | `22c6bdf...` |
| [39097001668](https://github.com/carlers/quantum-opt-dashboard/actions/runs/39097001668) | **665** | 13 | 790 | 790 | `07181560...` |
| [39097023374](https://github.com/carlers/quantum-opt-dashboard/actions/runs/39097023374) | **665** | 13 | 790 | 790 | `07181560...` |

All three still pass **30 tests** and reproduce the independent **SCIP** optimum, 15 fixed stations, candidate ordering and the *12-decimal numerical instance fingerprint* `806ef5072d79cd4e5cfde622b8b7a8a9175537aa78b18e34ff742e8f9159fce3`. The exact raw SCIP instance JSON digest differs between the first and later runs. The row-sum penalty also varies by one last-bit rounding step (`1.097819667627033` versus `1.0978196676270329`). The other two penalty settings produced the same 790 feasible draws, zero/one exact hits. **This correlation does not prove that a last-bit perturbation caused the difference**; it only establishes that both input drift and result drift occurred.

Accordingly, the **+8.984 pp** first-run feasibility contrasts and the corresponding confidence-style intervals are **not unique cross-run reproducible outputs**. In each of the two subsequent runs the difference was **+12.207 pp**, with respective midpoint/global descriptive 95% parent-block bootstrap intervals **[+8.105,+16.504] pp** and **[+7.715,+16.699] pp**. Exact-optimum hit differences remained −1.270 pp (midpoint) and −1.172 pp (global), though their resampling intervals also changed.

**Do not pool or choose among these reruns as independent preregistered repetitions.** The seeds are the same and the purpose of the reruns was code/documentation CI validation, not an independent fresh registered Monte Carlo design. The primary interpretation should retain all three archived runs, disclose this unresolved numerical/sampler reproducibility question, and investigate bit-level coefficient provenance under #24 **before asserting fixed-seed reproducibility across CI machines or publishing inferential performance comparisons**.
